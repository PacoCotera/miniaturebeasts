  4. 04

    Stay in the rain to collect Energy from struck stones, and risk a strike that breaks your Probe. Or shelter under a tree.

  5. 05

    A pod is sealed. It names its species if you’ve met it, otherwise “unknown species”. What’s inside waits for the Station.

  6. 06

    Send the cargo home

    Then the world turns: creatures roam and breed, storms leave charged stones, new finds appear. Nothing simply refills.

No timers. The world moves only when you move or act, so you can stop and think. Designed for the next build: a Call button that pulses, lures and pins; a Shield the storm wears down; Probe tiers that reach further into the fog.

Back at the Station

Open the pod.
Decide what it becomes.

One pod makes one mibi. Hatch it as it is, or study it first and choose from what it already carries. Designed, not built yet

  1. Identify

    Find out the pod’s species. That’s enough to hatch it unchanged; you never have to decode it all.

  2. Research, if you want

    Each study shows something this pod carries, like a hidden marking. What you learn stays learned.

  3. Create

    Keep the pod as it is, or set a trait you’ve researched, using only what this pod really carries.

  4. Incubate and open

    The mibi you meet is the one you made. No rerolls.

Real genetics, under the fur

Small creatures.
Real inheritance.

A mibi’s looks and abilities come from its genome, and the genes follow real inheritance.

You learn it by looking and breeding, not by reading.

One marking, one species

Two plain parents.
One surprise.

Pip’s pale markings are recessive. A plain Pip can carry pale without showing it. Breed two carriers and about one child in four comes out marked.

  • A pod can only become what it carries. Knowing about pale from another pod doesn’t put it in this one.
  • Breeding happens within a species, and every child is a new individual with two real parents.
  • Genomes start small and grow more complex as you find rarer pods.
  • For the curious: the genome is the seed for a mibi’s body, markings, movement and behaviour.

A few fair questions.

Is it real?

The game is being designed and built now. The exploration prototype is real and playable in your browser. The device pictures are concept art: nothing physical has been built yet, and the plan is to prove the game in software and playtest it before making boards and cases.

When can I get one?

There’s no date and no price. Follow the roadmap to see what’s being built and what comes next.

Can I build one myself?

Not yet, because the hardware isn’t designed. When it is, the boards and cases will be published as open hardware, to be printed and assembled with common tools. The software is open source already.

Do I need to know genetics?

No. Children can play by looking, comparing and breeding. The real framework underneath is there for anyone who wants to dig in.
