# The whole-screen gate: recalibration

Creative director, 2026-10-15 20:35 (America/Mexico_City). The owner rejected four screens I passed: Cargo, Incubator ("grow"), Probe and Create ((recorded decision), 10-15 20:29). Home was accepted.

## 1. What I missed

The cause I share across all four: I judged each screen against the fix lists and my own earlier review, and passed it when the fixes held and it was "better than main". I did not lay the owner's references beside it, although my contract names them as the bar ((audit record), (recorded decision)).

- **Cargo.** I passed the sage military cases on a painted bay as finished objects. The owner's references for the cargo containers are **(owner reference set) ((recorded decision) 10-10 11:28 and 11:31: "for the containers in the cargo haul you unload"). They show glass bottles with a cord loop and a cap, a yellow sphere with a lens and a rope loop, a matte jar with an orange band, a rugged yellow case with black corners and a handle, and white handheld units with teal accents and a loop handle. The owner also said "the crates may be a bit too rustic" (10-09 17:28) and, at 10-10 11:42 and 11:48, that each container shows what it holds: energy as a battery cell, data as a cartridge, essence as a vial. I never opened the folder. The build's bay shows near-identical green cases, and the haul items are small extras beside them.
- **Incubator ("grow").** I checked the arc, the plaque and the light, and passed it. I missed two things. First, the machinery is drawn flat (sage slabs, vector gauges), while the board's frame 3 and the rest of the Station are rendered, lit objects with depth: "doesn't follow the visual style, looks flat". Second, the margins: the left column of units, the gauges and the plaque sit at uneven distances from the bezel, and the composition does not hold a grid. I did not measure margins at all.
- **Probe.** I passed it because the Mend and Upgrade modules sat in the housing. The owner's Probe is a hand-held detector, held upright ((recorded decision) 10-10 11:33 and 11:57, the target (owner reference set) "a tall antenna"; the approved Probe concept). The bench lays it on its side in a horizontal cradle. I checked the module, not whether the object is shown as it is used.
- **Create.** I passed it under option A, with conditions about the choice reading. I missed that everything floats: the pod on a disc, the founder in a box, the chamber and the stamp case are each placed on a plain brown plane, with no bench, no surface and no contact. I also missed that the screen does not read as a place where you tweak genes. I judged the parts against the creative director's items and never asked the purpose question at screen level.

## 2. What the gate checks from now on

A screen passes only if the answer to every line is yes, judged on the whole 1× capture beside the references. "Better than before" is not a pass; that test belongs to merges (X29), not to the owner gate.

1. **References first.** For each screen I list every owner reference that bears on it ((recorded decision), the quality reference, the (owner reference set) folders, the concept frame) and lay each one beside the capture. Every object named in a reference is judged against that reference. If a reference I need is missing, I ask before passing.
2. **Objects as they are used.** Each device or object is shown in the pose and orientation the owner described: the Probe upright as held, containers that show what they carry.
3. **One rendered style.** Every object is rendered at the board's depth, lit from the top left and shaded. Flat or vector objects beside rendered ones fail.
4. **Grounded.** Every object sits on a surface or in a recess with contact and shadow. Nothing floats on an empty plane.
5. **Margins and grid.** Outer margins are even, and objects align to a grid. I measure the gaps to the bezel and between modules.
6. **Purpose at a glance.** In three seconds a stranger can say what this screen is for (Create: shaping a creature's genes). If not, it fails, whatever its parts.
7. **The owner's own words.** Each screen is checked against every line of the quality reference that names it.

## 4. Added 2026-10-17: why the gate passed the slip, and two new checks

**What I missed.** The owner rejected the slip on Create (and so on the Incubator): "doesn't read like part of the machine, or anything else" and "a wall of text that nobody will read". My art reviews passed it three times:
- `results/review-art-2026-10-16b.md` counted the filled slip as answering "wasted space";
- `results/review-art-2026-10-17a.md` and `results/review-art-2026-10-17b.md` flagged only its colour and one overlong line.

Three causes:
1. **I judged the text as "stand-in content, the build's".** I never asked whether the design should hold that much text at all. The stand-ins were at the spec's longest case, so they are the design.
2. **The owner's 13:26 words ("not sold on that cream color and lots of text") were applied only to the decision panels.** The slip is a cream panel of text, and I didn't carry the owner's words to every panel.
3. **No check asked what the object is in the world.** The slip passed "grounded" (it is clipped to the bench) without being any thing at all.

**The gate's checks 8 and 9, from now on, on every screen:**

8. **Words load.**
   - **Count the words** visible at 1× in the state's longest case, outside the top bar and the bottom line.
   - **What fails:** any object holding more than **6 words**, or any list of **3 or more lines of words**. Exceptions are a page whose purpose is reading (the Library's Book) and a budget the owner has approved.
   - **Prefer a picture or mark:** where a picture or a mark the screen already uses (a notch, a frost, a chip) could say it, a word that says it fails.
   - **Stand-in text counts:** text at the spec's longest case is the design, not "the build's".
9. **What it is in the world.**
   - Every panel, card or box is named as a thing: a part of the device (a plate cut from the housing, a module), or an object of the world that exists elsewhere in the game (a tag, a book, a crate).
   - A generic box, card or text panel fails, however well grounded.
   - The owner's words about one panel are applied to every panel of the same kind.

## 5. Added 2026-10-17: material matches behaviour

**What I missed.** For the slip I proposed the pod's paper tag, carrying marks that change as the player turns the drums. The owner: "a paper tag is not interactive, it makes no sense having a paper tag that changes with user interaction." I weighed object continuity (the tag travels with the pod) over behaviour. Continuity can't beat behaviour.

**Check 10, from now on, on every screen:**

10. **Material matches behaviour.** For each object, ask what it does over time, and whether its material can do that:
    - anything that updates live with the player's input, or with time, is a display, a lamp, a moving part or a living thing: something that can change;
    - paper, labels, engraving and painted marks stay fixed while on screen;
    - an object whose material can't do what the object does fails, however well it reads.

## 6. Added 2026-10-17 (T-20261016-168): the owner's bar comes first

**What I missed.** The owner rejected the Return panel, the Cargo report and the Incubator (06:38), all passed by this gate. Each of them met its spec and its fix list, and I judged each one alone. The owner's diagnosis (06:41): "nobody looking at the integrated experience. and small adjustments keep being approved, compounding to a mess of UI." Spec compliance was the first test, and it should have been the last.

**The gate, from now on, in this order. A screen fails at the first no:**

1. **In the device sheet, never alone.**
   - The proof is placed in the device sheet (`tools/device-sheet.py`), among its neighbours in flow order, at 1×.
   - It must share the device's one ground, one frame, one pane, one pod and one stamp (`specs/station-experience-review.md` §2).
   - A screen that is right alone and foreign in the sheet fails.
2. **The two-second first look.**
   - Shown the 1× capture for two seconds, someone who has not read the specs can say what the screen is for and what to look at first.
   - The game designer's three-second purpose test (`specs/purpose-briefs-b20261017-1.md` §5) follows it.
3. **Every owner verbatim on file for the screen.**
   - Each line is listed, with how the proof answers it.
   - One unanswered line fails the screen.
   - The owner's words about one panel or object apply to every panel or object of the same kind (check 9).
4. **The Miniature Lives look.**
   - The proof sits beside `design/art-direction.md` and the guide's plate 01: the life leads, painted and lit from the top left; the machine is quiet slate.
   - If the screen does not read as Miniature Lives, it fails, whatever else holds.
5. **Spec and guide compliance**, which is checks 1–10 above and the guide's sections cited by the ticket. These come last: they confirm a proof that already passed 1–4. They never rescue one that failed.

**How approvals work:**
- **No piecemeal approvals.** Small fixes are batched per screen family and judged together, in the sheet.
- **A patch on a patch fails.** A screen that comes back a second time for the same point goes back to concept. It does not get another patch.
- **Nothing reaches the owner** without this gate's verdict and the game design gate's, both in `results/`.
