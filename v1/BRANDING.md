# Branding and attribution

## Product and creature names

The product is **Miniature Beasts**. A creature is a **mibi**; the plural is
**mibis**. Use the full product name with spaces in player-facing prose.
These names preserve the balance of creature companionship, exploration,
science and social connection.

The project website is [miniaturebeasts.com](https://miniaturebeasts.com/).
The [genome workbench](https://miniaturebeasts.com/genome/) and
[sandbox](https://miniaturebeasts.com/sandbox/) share that origin.

The home device is **Station**; see
[device terminology](specs/devices.md#device-terminology). Existing source paths,
repository identifiers and original reference artwork may still use Critter Lab or Lab.
Current screen titles, composed review exports and product presentations use
Miniature Beasts and Station, including when they reuse earlier artwork. Preserve
original source bytes and attribution separately from those current compositions.
Legacy identifiers do not reopen the naming decision or change mechanics.

## Current identity and attribution

Miniature Beasts and Dirty Pawz Press identify the project's official offerings. Critter Lab is the former product name retained in earlier sources and notices. The source licenses do not grant trademark permission or imply endorsement. This policy makes no claim that these names are registered trademarks.

You may truthfully credit the project, discuss it, and describe derivation or compatibility. For example: “Based on Miniature Beasts; independently manufactured and supported by Example.” Preserve the attribution and notices required by the applicable licenses.

Use a distinct name and presentation for independent kits, services and forks so customers can identify who manufactures, operates and supports them. Obtain permission before claiming official status or endorsement.

This policy does not add copyright restrictions, override lawful descriptive use or statutory rights, remove required attribution, or revoke the copyright license for artwork containing a mark.
