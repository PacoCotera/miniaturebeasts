## Blocking

1. **The gene pictures are crops, not looks** (the owner: "you don't know what you're looking at", "lazy design").
   - Most tiles are hard-edged rectangular slices of the founder: a strip of flank, half a leg, the back with a bar of empty well under it (`-cd-tiles-4x.png`).
   - At 48 px a first-time viewer can't say which look a tile is, or how it differs from its neighbour. Eight of the looks are like this.
   - A gene picture must be **the look itself, framed to read**: the markings as a patch of patterned coat, the eyes as a pair of eyes, the tail as a tail. Each is centred and lit in its socket, with no crop edge.
   - **Gap and owner:** painted close-ups per look are needed, and the composite rule (signed art only) can't make them. This is new painting for the art director. If it needs paid generation, the budget is the lead's to raise with the owner. Don't fill it with crops.
2. **The candled form is pasted, not lit through the shell** (the owner: "a weird egg overimposed").
   - Inside the shell, the known head is a flat colour cut-out on a white block silhouette (`-cd-candled-3x.png`). It reads as a sticker on the pod, not a form inside it.
   - Candling must look like light passing through: the form a soft, warm silhouette, with its known parts tinted by the light coming through the shell, the shell's own pattern over it, and the edges diffused by the shell's thickness.
   - Unknown parts are frost within that glow, not a white cut-out.
3. **Three rows are empty** (Movement, Stamina, Character). Empty heads and wells are three eighths of the rack and read as unfinished.
   - This is honest, because the behaviour moments aren't painted yet. But a proof for the owner can't show it.
   - Either the behaviour pictures are painted (the game designer's brief §1: one moment per end of each doing), or the proof uses a species and state where those rows are honestly sealed or still a surprise, drawn with their register marks.
4. **The scan light doesn't read.** The mint light on the founder's eyes vanishes on a teal creature. The body link is the screen's key legibility idea (review §3.2), so it must be visible on every coat: a light that is brighter than the coat, or a soft wash with a clear edge. The art director proposes how.
