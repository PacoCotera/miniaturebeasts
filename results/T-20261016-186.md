## In one line

**Home gives the feel everything and the mechanics nothing, and the one bridge between them, the individual mibi, isn't drawn.** All four critiques found the first two halves. ## Where they disagree, and the call

1. **How residents become individuals.** These answers are not contradictory; they are three layers of one answer:
   - the genome engineer: draw each one as itself, always;
   - the game designer: names on events only;
   - the art director: the life carries its news (grown larger, a first's triangle on the animal).

   **Call:** the drawing always differs, since the genome engineer is right that it is the root. Labels and marks appear only on focus or on an event, which keeps the game designer's "a scene at rest". Nothing is laid over the window permanently.
2. **The column: grow by state, or keep a fixed place.** The art director wants the module that needs you to grow while quiet ones shrink. The game designer keeps a fixed place and order, learnt once ("one machine").
   - **Call:** fixed places and order, with no reflow.
   - Inside its pane, the waiting module's subject comes forward (lit, larger, its amber lamp), and quiet modules dim to their object in its well. This gives the art director's "a machine that answers state" without breaking "learnt once".
3. **What the bottom line's spare slot holds.** Three proposals:
   - the game designer: the next step toward the pursuit;
   - the genome engineer: one genetic sentence when it is the best next move;
   - UI/UX: a standing open question.

   **Call:** one slot holds the player's thread, the next step toward their pursuit, worded in the genome's own words when the best move is genetic. It never repeats what ✓ does (UI/UX's one message per slot). Whether an open question belongs on Home at all is the game designer's.
4. **Joining the window to the machine** (the art director: feed lines and a chute along the hand-offs). Only one critique proposes it, and it could slide into "equipment for show". **Call:** worth a concept, but only where the line carries a real hand-off (the pod carry, the hatch walk-in). It is judged in the sheet; it is not a rule yet.
5. **The world turn "☀ 5".** The game designer says remove it or tie it to something readable; UI/UX says label it or remove it. **Call:** remove it unless it shows what changed in the world. A bare number fails both motivations.
