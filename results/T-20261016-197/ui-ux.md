## 1. The hand-off: the objects and their destinations do the telling

There are no paths, no dots, no trails and no "in flight". Each thing's journey is told by four beats:

1. **The destination answers first.** Before anything moves, the place it is going to responds:
   - for a material, its mark in the top bar brightens;
   - for a pod, the Pods hatch slides open in the bay's side wall and shows its free wells.

   The eye has the end point before the journey starts.
2. **The thing itself travels, whole.**
   - It lifts from where it lay and moves on one eased arc. The art director's carry row in guide §9 sets the timing; no figure is chosen here.
   - A material token shrinks as it goes into the machine.
   - A pod keeps its size, because it is the same pod the player meets next in Pods (the flow pass's carry: one shell, the same object).
3. **The arrival answers.**
   - The counter ticks with the house flash. The flash shows the same "+n" the token wore on the floor, so the number at each end is the link.
   - The pod's well lights `mint` as it settles.
4. **The source empties.** What has left is gone from the floor. When the crate is empty it slides back into the queue, dimmed (`05`). The queue then reads as the haul, taken in order.

**Order within a crate:**
- The materials go first, together, a beat apart, all to the top bar. They are small and fast.
- Then the pods go one at a time, each its own moment. They are the thread.
- After the last pod the hatch closes. If Pods is full, the hatch stays shut with its amber lamp and the pod waits sealed on the floor. That is a fact, not a loss; the game designer words it, if it needs words.

**Why this reads without a caption:** the destination moves before the object, and the same object or number arrives there. Nothing on screen needs a note to be understood (§7, "nothing only a caption explains").

## 2. Focus

- **No ring on Cargo.** The screen has one action, and the haul plays by itself. Nothing is chosen, so nothing is focused, as on Home's room focus.
- **The queue is the count.** The waiting crates sit in a row and the current one has left the row. There is no "Crate 2 of 3".

## 3. The bottom line (minimal)

| Moment | ✓ | Middle | ← |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| At rest, with crates waiting | **Open them all** ("Open it" for one crate) | empty | Home |
| While a crate plays | **Skip**: cuts this crate's hand-off to its end (everything arrived); the next crate starts (the panel spec's cut 4, the skip rule) | empty | Home |
| After the last crate, with pods arrived | **Look at the pods**: Pods, with the ring on the newest pod | empty | Home |
| After the last crate, with no pods | none (no ✓ cap) | empty | Home |

- **Gone:**
  - the context "any key: the next crate" (the crates play by themselves; ✓ skips);
  - the place word ("from the pond"): the crate's place badge is a picture, so it isn't said twice;
  - any totals panel: the counters already show what came.
- **← Home** at every moment, as T-181 row 1. ← during a crate first lets that crate finish as a cut, so nothing is half-taken.

## 4. Asks

- **The art director:**
  - where the Pods hatch sits in the whole-screen composition (the side wall nearest Pods' left wall keeps the flow pass's left-to-right journey);
  - the queue's place on the floor;
  - how the destination's "answer" is painted: a mark brightening, a door opening.
- **The game designer:** what a crate holds and where each goes; the full-Pods wording, if any; "Open them all", "Skip" and "Look at the pods".
