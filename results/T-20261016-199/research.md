# Home, from research (T-20261016-199, stage 1)

A shared file. UI/UX writes **Structure** (priority, state at a glance, the route into each system). The art director writes **Look** (how the good ones read as a place where you run things, and how ours stays Miniature Lives) in their own section below.

**Images:** these are commercial games, so the entries use precise descriptions and links rather than embedded screenshots. The links are for checking the description, not endorsements of every claim on those pages.

---

## Structure (UI/UX)

For each reference:
- **Priority:** what is biggest, and why.
- **State:** what you can tell at a glance, without opening anything.
- **Routes:** how you get into each system.
- **Teaches:** the lesson, and how it applies to us.

### 1. Fallout Shelter: the base is the interface

- **What it is:** a side-on cut through the vault, with rooms stacked in a grid that fills the screen. There is no separate menu of systems: each room is its system, drawn where it sits.
- **Priority:** whatever has a **bubble** over it. A room that has finished producing shows its resource icon floating in its middle. A room in trouble (fire, raiders) flashes. Size follows how much you built, not importance, so the eye is pulled by the bubbles, not by area.
- **State:** each room shows its workers, its production bar and its bubble. The top bar holds the three stores, each as a gauge, so "low power" is visible from anywhere.
- **Routes:** tap the room. Zoom carries you into it; there is no screen change.
- **Teaches:**
  - State lives on the thing, not in a list. A "ready" signal is a picture of what you will get, sitting on its source.
  - What doesn't need you is quiet but still visible. Nothing is hidden to save space.
  - The flip side: with many rooms, the screen becomes a field of small things. Area tells you nothing.
- **For us:** the "ready" sign (crates, a bud) should be the reward itself, shown on its module. Home must still decide area by loop weight, because Fallout Shelter shows that leaving area to build order makes priority invisible.
- **Links:**
  - [Fallout Shelter rooms (wiki)](https://fallout.wiki/wiki/Fallout_Shelter_rooms): production pools and collecting.
  - [Walkthrough](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Fallout-Shelter/walkthrough/3): "a green icon floating in the middle" to collect.

### 2. FTL: Faster Than Light: one ship, every system's state in one bar

- **What it is:** a landscape screen. The ship in cross-section fills the middle, with the crew visible in its rooms. Along the bottom left is a row of system icons, each with a vertical stack of power bars.
- **Priority:** the ship (the subject) is biggest. The systems are a compact strip, but **each strip is always live**. The danger state (a damaged bar, a fire in a room) is on the ship itself.
- **State:** each system's bar does three jobs at once: how much power it has, how much it is using and how damaged it is (the developers' own design goal: "as easily explained and visible to the player as possible"). One glance reads every system's health.
- **Routes:** the system icon is the control. Clicking it powers it up or down; there is no screen behind it.
- **Teaches:**
  - A dense status strip beside a big subject works when every mark carries real state.
  - The strip's size is small but its information is total. The subject's size is big because it is where things happen.
- **For us:** a module's picture must carry its true state, the way an FTL bar does: how many pods are unread, how full the guide is. If a module can't show state it doesn't deserve space. That answers the owner's "the incubator takes a lot of space just to show emptiness".
- **Links:**
  - [The making of FTL, chapter 2](https://episodiccontentmag.com/2015/06/10/anything-but-sports-the-making-of-ftl-chapter-2-of-4/): the developers on the power bars.
  - [FTL wiki: Ship](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Ship).

### 3. Oxygen Not Included, and Tiny Tower: the priority stack

- **What it is:**
  - In ONI, a column of alerts at the screen's edge ("Duplicant is starving", "Low oxygen") over a busy colony view.
  - In Tiny Tower (a tall phone screen of stacked floors), small coloured icons pop up in the corner: brown when a floor needs restocking, red when the lift is crowded. Tapping one scrolls you to the floor.
- **Priority:** a separate, ordered list of what needs you, apart from the world view. Tiny Tower's is tiny (one or two icons) and colour-coded by kind. ONI's grows long, and players call it noisy.
- **State:** you see what needs action. Everything else you must inspect.
- **Routes:** the alert is the route; it jumps you to its source.
- **Teaches:**
  - A short, ordered "needs you" queue is the cheapest way to say what matters most now, and it can be separate from the layout.
  - It must stay short. ONI shows what happens when every system is allowed a voice: an alarm wall.
  - Tiny Tower also shows that letting things wait costs nothing ("you aren't penalised for letting stock run out"), which fits our "nothing negative" ruling.
- **For us:** the one ✓ that "does what needs you" is our version, and it works. It only fails when the layout contradicts it, giving the Probe a big box while the Library, the engine, is small at the bottom. Keep a single ordered need. Let area follow loop weight, and let the need follow urgency.
- **Links:**
  - [ONI versions (wiki)](https://oxygennotincluded.wiki.gg/wiki/Versions/R1-260344): priority and alert changes.
  - [Klei forum, "important tasks"](https://forums.kleientertainment.com/forums/topic/93483-important-tasks/).
  - [Pocket Gamer, Tiny Tower beginners' guide](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/031220/r/): the brown and red icons.

### 4. Pocket Frogs (NimbleBit): a breeding game whose home is the habitats

- **What it is:** a phone game about breeding frogs for colour and pattern. Home is a habitat: a small square of pond and lily pads with your frogs sitting in it. You swipe between habitats, and one is the **nursery**, where eggs hatch into tadpoles and then frogs. A few buttons along the edge lead to the store, the pond (catching flies for food) and, in older versions, the **Catalog**, the collection book of every frog type.
- **Priority:** the frogs in their habitat, at full screen. Breeding is done by dragging one frog onto another, on the home screen itself.
- **State:** the nursery shows its eggs and tadpoles as things, growing. A habitat that is full is visibly full.
- **Routes:** the main mechanic (breeding) happens on Home itself, with no route needed. Secondary systems are edge buttons.
- **Teaches:**
  - The closest genre neighbour lets the core verb happen where the creatures live. The collection (the Catalog) was the long-term engine. Notably it was later removed, and players missed it: the collection is what gives breeding a direction.
  - "Nursery overflow" (eggs go to the next habitat when the nursery is full) is a no-loss rule like our sittings queue.
- **For us:**
  - The Library (the guide filling up, the wish) is the direction of the loop, as Pocket Frogs' Catalog was. It belongs where the eye lands, not at the bottom.
  - Our pods correspond to their nursery eggs. Show them as the household's future, all of them, by a stated rule, not "three pods" with no criterion.
- **Links:**
  - [Pocket Frogs (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_Frogs): habitats, nursery, the Catalog's removal.
  - [TUAW preview](https://www.engadget.com/2010-09-14-tuaw-preview-nimblebits-pocket-frogs.html).
  - [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pocket-frogs-tiny-pond-keeper/id386644958): nursery overflow.

### 5. Neko Atsume: a cozy home where the collection is the point

- **What it is:** a single yard on a porch, seen from above, with five spots for goodies and a food bowl. Cats arrive while you're away. A short menu (Cats, Goodies, Shop, Album) sits at the edge, and the **Catbook** records every cat that has visited.
- **Priority:** the yard is the whole screen, and the cats are the event. The food bowl is the one maintenance thing, shown as a bowl that empties.
- **State:**
  - Which cats are here (the picture).
  - Whether food is left (the bowl).
  - Whether a gift was left (fish appear in the yard).
- **Routes:** the menu at the edge. The Catbook is one press away, and each new cat sends you there.
- **Teaches:**
  - Cozy works when maintenance is one small object (the bowl), the picture is the reward, and the collection book is the reason to return. Each visit asks "who came?", then "is that one new?", then the book.
  - There is no column of modules. The functions are a quiet menu, because the yard and the book carry the game.
- **For us:** the move from feel to mechanics in Neko Atsume is "a new cat → the book". Ours could be "a new look on a resident or pod → the Library". Home should show the book's progress where the life is, not as a module among five.
- **Links:**
  - [Neko Atsume wiki: game menu](https://nekoatsume.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Menu).
  - [Goodies](https://nekoatsume.fandom.com/wiki/Goodies).
  - [iMore guide](https://imore.com/neko-atsume-game-guide-how-collect-all-cats): the Catbook.

### 6. Animal Crossing: New Horizons: the NookPhone, and the Critterpedia as an engine

- **What it is:** the island is the game. Systems sit behind the NookPhone, a grid of app icons (Camera, Nook Miles, DIY Recipes, Critterpedia, Map, Passport, …) called up over the world. The **Critterpedia** lists every bug, fish and sea creature: the caught ones are drawn, the missing ones are silhouettes, and a donated one carries an owl mark.
- **Priority:** the world. The phone is a lens you raise, not a permanent panel.
- **State:**
  - The Critterpedia shows the collection's gaps as silhouettes, so "what's missing" is visible as shapes.
  - Nook Miles tasks show progress bars.
  - Nothing about systems is on the world screen except a small badge.
- **Routes:** one button raises the phone, and each app is one icon in a fixed grid.
- **Teaches:**
  - The collection drives the daily loop because the gaps are drawn. The silhouette is the hook: you know there is something and roughly what shape it is, but not what.
  - The fixed grid is learnt once, so routes are cheap, but it carries little state.
- **For us:**
  - The Library's state on Home should be gaps, not a closed book: the wish's pieces as silhouettes, lit as they are found. That is the engine, visibly turning.
  - Our sealed chapters and unread marks are already silhouettes in spirit.
- **Links:**
  - [Nookipedia: NookPhone](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/NookPhone).
  - [Critterpedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Critterpedia).

### 7. Tamagotchi (Uni and the classics): one creature, a ring of care icons

- **What it is:** a tiny screen, mostly the creature. On the classics, a ring of 8–10 small care icons (food, light, play, medicine, bathroom, meter, discipline, call) frames the screen's top and bottom. The **attention icon** lights when the pet needs you. The Uni adds rooms and a three-button control.
- **Priority:** the creature, always. The icons are small and fixed in place, learnt once.
- **State:**
  - The creature's own pose and the attention icon carry need.
  - The meter (hunger, happiness) is one press away, not on the main screen.
- **Routes:** step through the fixed ring with one button and confirm with another. It is the same input as ours: few keys, a fixed order.
- **Teaches:**
  - On a small device with few keys, a **fixed ring of routes** (never reflowed) plus **one attention signal** is enough.
  - The creature carries the feel; the ring carries the functions without competing.
  - Its weakness: the ring has equal-weight icons, so importance isn't shown. That is the owner's complaint about our five equal boxes.
- **For us:** keep fixed places (the owner's 11:27), but don't make them equal. A fixed order with unequal sizes, in rank order, keeps "learnt once" and shows weight.
- **Links:**
  - [Siliconera on the Tamagotchi Uni](https://www.siliconera.com/tamagotchi-uni-is-surprisingly-good-at-being-a-wearable-virtual-pet/): three buttons, rooms.

### 8. Spiritfarer: a hub you build, where each building is a system

- **What it is:** your boat is the hub. A side view shows buildings stacked on deck: a garden, a kitchen, a loom, a foundry, and the spirits' houses. You walk to a building to use it. The boat grows as you upgrade it.
- **Priority:** the spirits (the creatures you care for) and their houses are the emotional centre. Production buildings are where you spend time.
- **State:** each building shows its state by its own animation (the garden growing, the kitchen smoking). A spirit's mood shows over its head.
- **Routes:** walk there. The boat's layout is the menu, and players arrange it so that sequences of use sit together.
- **Teaches:**
  - A hub reads as "the place where you run things" when the systems are machines in one connected space, not separate cards.
  - Things used in sequence sit next to each other: garden → kitchen; for us, Pods → Create → Incubator.
- **For us:** the hand-off order (Cargo → Pods → Create → Incubator → Vivarium) could be the spatial order on Home, so the layout is the loop. Today the column is a list in no particular order of use.
- **Links:**
  - [Spiritfarer wiki: boat buildings](https://spiritfarer.fandom.com/wiki/Boat_Buildings).
  - [Game Informer preview](https://www.gameinformer.com/preview/2020/07/30/why-spiritfarer-is-a-different-kind-of-sim).

### 9. Teenage Engineering OP-1: a small landscape device whose screen is a picture of each mode

- **What it is:** a portable synthesizer with a 320 × 160 landscape OLED and four colour-coded encoders. Each mode (each synth engine, each sequencer) is drawn as its own small scene, not a parameter list. The encoders' colours match the on-screen elements they turn.
- **Priority:** the current mode fills the screen. Switching modes is by dedicated hardware keys, so there is no on-screen menu.
- **State:** the picture is the state: a tape reel turning, a waveform's shape, the "twin gorillas" drum sequencer moving in time.
- **Routes:** physical keys, each with one mode. "Wordy parameters and complex menu systems are replaced with imaginative scenes."
- **Teaches:**
  - On a small landscape device, a system can be a picture that is also a readout.
  - The routes can live on the hardware (our coloured room keys) and leave the screen free.
- **For us:**
  - We have room keys (Home, Vivarium, Research, Library). Home doesn't have to spend its area on routes the keys already give.
  - Home's area can go to state and the one thing that needs you, which is where the owner says ours fails.
- **Links:**
  - [OP-1 (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Engineering_OP-1).
  - [MusicRadar review](https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/teenage-engineering-op-1).
  - [Soundtech guide](https://www.soundtech.co.uk/musicians-blog/our-guide-to-the-teenage-engineering-op-1.html).

### 10. Slime Rancher: a ranch of plots, each a system with its state on it

- **What it is:** first-person, so not a screen layout. The ranch is a set of plots (corrals, gardens, coops, silos), each upgraded in place: walls, an auto-feeder, a plort collector. Later the drones automate the chores.
- **Priority:** what you walk past. Problem plots announce themselves physically (slimes escaping, an empty feeder).
- **State:** on the plot: the feeder's fill, the collector's stock.
- **Routes:** walk to it.
- **Teaches:**
  - Upgrades that remove chores (auto-feeder, collector, drones) shift the player from upkeep to collecting. The hub gets calmer as you progress.
  - The hub's attention should fall on what is new, not on upkeep.
- **For us:** chores (opening crates, the Probe) should shrink on Home as the game goes on, and discovery (the Library, unread pods) should grow. That argues against a Probe given prime space.
- **Links:**
  - [Slime Rancher wiki: the Ranch](https://slimerancher.fandom.com/wiki/The_Ranch).
  - [Walkthrough](https://almarsguides.com/computer/games/slimerancher/walkthrough/part2/): corral upgrades, the plort collector.

## What the references agree on (UI/UX)

1. **Area follows meaning, not inventory.**
   - FTL gives the subject the space and the systems a dense strip.
   - Tamagotchi's equal icons are its known weakness.
   - Fallout Shelter shows that leaving area to build order hides priority.
   - **For Home:** area by loop weight (the game designer's stage-2 ranking). The owner's verdict: the Library is the engine and the Probe is minor.
2. **The state is drawn on the thing, and it is real state.**
   - FTL's bar, Fallout Shelter's bubble, Neko Atsume's bowl, the Critterpedia's silhouettes.
   - A module that shows nothing (the empty Incubator, the closed Cargo door, a closed book) has no claim on space.
   - **For Home:** each module's picture must answer "what is the state?", or it shrinks to a mark.
3. **One short, ordered "needs you".**
   - Tiny Tower's two icons and Tamagotchi's one attention icon, as opposed to ONI's wall.
   - **For Home:** our single ✓ already does this. Keep it, and make sure the layout agrees with it.
4. **The collection is the engine, and its gaps are drawn.**
   - The Critterpedia's silhouettes, the Catbook, Pocket Frogs' Catalog (missed when it was removed).
   - **For Home:** the Library shows gaps and the wish's pieces, lit or not, and gets primary space.
5. **The core verb happens near the life.**
   - Pocket Frogs breeds on Home. Neko Atsume's reward is the yard.
   - **For Home:** the move from the window to depth (T-191, on hold) and the pursuit stay central.
6. **Routes can live on the hardware and in order of use.**
   - The OP-1's mode keys; Spiritfarer's deck laid out by sequence.
   - **For Home:** the room keys already route. Home's layout can follow the loop's order (pod → shape → grow → live → record) instead of listing destinations.
7. **The hub calms as chores are automated** (Slime Rancher).
   - **For Home:** upkeep (crates, the Probe) gets small and transient. Discovery gets the standing space.

## Structural families these suggest for stage 3 (input, not the sketches)

These are four genuinely different structures, to be tested against the game designer's ranking:

- **A. Subject and strip** (FTL): the living window wide, with a dense live-state strip on one edge where each system is a readout, sized by rank, with the Library largest. No boxes of equal size.
- **B. The book is home** (Neko Atsume with the Critterpedia): the Library's spread (the wish and the guide's gaps) is the main surface. The living window is a smaller inset of who is home. Upkeep is a small queue.
- **C. The station in section** (Fallout Shelter with Spiritfarer): one cutaway of the Station, its rooms laid out in the loop's order, each room showing its own state on itself. The window is one of the rooms, the largest.
- **D. The life, with a lens** (Tamagotchi with the NookPhone): the living window full-screen, a single "needs you" signal, and the systems raised as an overlay on demand, in a fixed ring sized by rank.
