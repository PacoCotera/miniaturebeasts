## The call: what an unread trait draws

The two proposals disagree:
- **The art director:** "the typical look": crest, plain, eyes between.
- **The genome engineer:** the stand as painted today: plain, leaf crest, wide pale rings.

**The call: an unread trait draws the species' most common look on pods.** That is the look a player is least likely to be misled by. For the Loika it is plain (78%), leaf crest (70%) and eyes between (46%), which is the art director's base. "Wide pale rings" is only 27% and would mislead more often. The genome engineer's check stays: each default must be a look the pool can produce. Add one more: it is the modal look in the measured pod share (`stand-looks.json`). As the genome engineer proposes, nothing on Home veils it; the glance marks the chapter unread.

## The concern: the Loika has the least to show

The genome engineer is honest: four random Loikas all look different only **35%** of the time, because the Loika has three look traits. The genome must not be stretched to fake it, and I agree: look-alikes are true.

But this is a game design problem, not an art one. The Loika is the household in every Home golden and, today, likely the player's first species. The cozy motivation's "which one is mine?" fails most often on exactly the species a new player meets first. Deeper species close the gap on their own (S03 98%, S12 95%).

This goes to the lead and the owner as a design question, not a fix for this ticket:
- (a) give the Loika one or two more visible look traits in its frame (the genome engineer's to cost);
- (b) make a deeper species the first one a player meets;
- (c) accept it, with names on focus (T-191) carrying identity for look-alikes.

I lean to (a): the starter species should show the game's promise best, not least. It is the owner's call, since it changes a species.

**The owner's picture** uses four chosen pods that differ (seeds 12, 13, 14, 107). It must be labelled as chosen, so it doesn't promise that every household of four Loikas looks this varied.
