Species notes: the Peplos, a moth of the flowers. Only what differs from the house rendering.
Colour placement: the body colour on the head and the body; the second colour on the wings and the legs.
Eyes: its two compound eyes are drawn as the house eye, with a deep teal iris: round, wet, a lid line, two glints. Never insect eyes: no facets, no mesh, no black dot in a white ball.
Material: dusted translucent membranes on the wings, the second colour with visible veins a shade deeper, the body showing faintly through near the base; a fuzzy thorax; the rest of the body smooth. Its two touches: a powdery pale dust on the wing near its base, and a soft ring of fuzz at the collar between head and thorax.
Volume: the thorax is the fullest form, round behind the head; its top takes the highlight and its underside both shadow steps. The abdomen is soft segments, each turning from a lit top to a shadowed underside, narrowing to the tip. Each wing curls slightly along its leading edge, lit along the curl and shaded beneath it, so no wing is a flat sheet.
Parts: the head is round, with two feathered antennae that curl a little at their tips. The four wings lie folded along the body where Image 1 lays them, rounded at their tips, never a plank or a board. The six legs are fine and jointed, each ending in a small soft foot, never balls or pegs.
Posture: it perches lightly on its six feet, its head a little low and forward, its antennae up, its wings resting quietly.
Character: flighty and bright, a small flier of the flowers.
