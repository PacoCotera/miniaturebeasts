# Species and clan names: the first drop

**Proposal** from the copywriter, 2026-10-07, for the owner. It names the sixteen species and sixteen clans of [taxonomy](taxonomy.md) §3 (codes S01–S16, C01–C16) under its §5 rules, the 10-07 naming decisions (English experience; invented, Latin, Greek or less-heard roots; no Spanish diminutives; clean room) and the method of the [name screen](name-screen.md). Nothing here is picked yet. The codes stay in the frames until the owner chooses.

## The voice

The tome is written by a field naturalist for a child and the parent reading with them. It is warm, curious and exact, and it never coos. A name should sound found rather than made, like a word the naturalist heard where the creature lives and kept. Each name says one true thing about every member: a habit, a look, a place. It never names a look that varies between members. It has no pet suffix, no pun and no brand snap, and it is easy to say aloud to a five-year-old.

## How the names are built

- **Roots** come from Finnish, Basque, Latin, Greek, Georgian and Tupi. Each root is a plain everyday word in its own language, never a sacred or offensive one. Each name is a shortened or reshaped form, so it is not the dictionary word itself.
- **Species** have two or three syllables. **Clans** use one construction (see below). Within each set, no two names share a first syllable. No two share a closing rhyme such as -el, -ar or -on, and no two share a last syllable. Four species end in an open -a (Loika, Untuva, Igara, Nebla) and three in -o (Namio, Arrano, Kilpo), each on a different syllable (see question 1).
- **Banned:** -ín, -ito, -y and -ie, along with their sounds. Anything that reads as a drug, a brand or a Pokémon is also out (that is why Brontium, Leptra and Lampar were dropped).
- **Both languages:** every name was read for its Mexican Spanish meaning and slang. Three candidates were withdrawn this way: Tholoka (its ending reads *loca*), Perho (*perro*) and Kerdo (*cerdo*).

## The 16 clans

## Clearance

**Method.** For every candidate, the search tool returned titles and summaries, which were read for any existing creature, character, item, card, brand or product, and for common nouns or surnames that would swamp a search. **6** means the six searches were run one by one, each with the name in quotes: creature, monster, game, Pokémon, character, brand. **1** means one broad search: the name in quotes plus creature, character, game and brand (the engine reads OR as plain words, so it searches the name broadly), and sometimes an extra one. Around 140 candidates and 200 searches in all. The session's search limit stopped the six-search round after Loika and Untuva, with six other candidates rejected along the way. **The other 30 final names have one broad search each and need their six before anyone picks** (question 2). This is a web screen, not legal clearance (name screen §7).

## Questions for the owner

1. **Endings.** I read "no shared ending" as no shared last syllable and no shared closing rhyme. Four species end in an open -a and three in -o, each on a different syllable, and the clans share the classical -a on purpose. Is that acceptable, or should every species also end on a different vowel? The reserves allow the swap.
2. **Finishing clearance.** Loika and Untuva passed all six searches. The other 30 names had one broad search each before the session's search limit stopped the work. Should the remaining 180 searches run in a follow-up before you pick, and should a professional trademark search cover each name that ships (name screen §7)?
3. **The five cautions.** Krisel is a surname, Kauris a dictionary plural, Nebla is corrected to *nebula* by search engines, Elvar is close to Elvarg, and Phrakta sounds like "frak" when said aloud. Keep them, or swap now from the reserves?
