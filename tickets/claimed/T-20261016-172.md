## Owner (2026-10-17 06:50 UTC, verbatim)
"the game designer and the art director MUST write a detail spec for the visuals, styles, etc for the station and the companion. every builder/artist that gets tickets is going offrails inventing their own thing."
## Purpose
One document detailed enough that a builder or artist with a ticket never has to invent a look. If it isn't in the spec, they stop and ask. It replaces guesswork across both devices.
## Who
- The art director writes the visuals: palette, materials, light, rendering, components, icons and type.
- The game designer writes what each screen and element must communicate, and the rules a picture must follow: what is shown and when, the words budget, and how state, cost and outcome read.
- The creative director (session_01X8o4MPAufzzAzVG1o4dfxU) signs it as the UI/art gate. Their T-168 experience review is an input. The game designer signs the game design gate.
## Must contain, with a picture for every rule (labelled 1x PNG examples, a do beside a don't)
1. **The look in one page:** what the Station and the Companion are in the world, and how they relate to the Miniature Lives vivarium style.
2. **Palette:** exact colour values per layer and per role (housing, display, light, action, state: changed, clash, unknown, glint). Name the forbidden ones (no brown on brown, no cream panels).
3. **Materials and light:** housing, displays, plates and glass, each shown rendered. Light direction and glow rules.
4. **Components:** decision panel, card, display screen, plate, tile, badge, progress, the genome marks, buttons and key hints. Each with sizes, states, margins and a rendered example.
5. **Type:** the scale, where words may appear, and the words budget per element.
6. **Icons and marks:** the minimum legible size, the drawing style, and the rule that no meaning rides on a tiny glyph or an unexplained colour.
7. **Layout:** the grid, margins and density per screen family on 1024×600, and the Companion's screen equivalents.
8. **Screen families:** for each Station screen and the Companion's screens, its purpose sentence, what reads first, and its family's direction (from T-168).
9. **Motion and feedback:** the dock, reveals, presses and refusals.
10. **How to use it:** every visual ticket cites its sections; anything not covered is parked to the art director, never invented.
