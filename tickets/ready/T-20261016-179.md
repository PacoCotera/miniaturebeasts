## Purpose
A short, high-level game design guideline that the owner can read and judge, and that every designer, artist and builder can follow. It says what playing Miniature Beasts is like and why it holds people. The owner has said they are not a game design expert, so bring the expertise: name the patterns, say why each fits this game, and say what we deliberately avoid. This is the game-design counterpart of art-direction.md (T-178).
## Who
- The game designer writes it.
- The creative director signs the UI/art and experience gate. The genome engineer checks that nothing contradicts the framework.
- Then the owner reviews it.
## Shape
- Expand the top of design/game.md in place (who it is for, the loop, what a session feels like, design principles). Do not add a new document, and keep the mechanics table below as it is.
- A few pages, principle-led, no numbers. Cover:
  - the core loop and the longer arcs around it;
  - the hooks that promote engagement, and the ones we refuse (no punishing absence, no dark patterns);
  - the general feel of the game;
  - what shows progression, to the player and on the devices;
  - what makes you come back (short, daily, long term);
  - interactions across the three devices: the Companion, the Station and the Caddy;
  - how decisions are communicated (options, cost, outcome, commitment);
  - what it is not.
- One or two references per principle are welcome (genre examples by name, described, never copied), plus our own accepted screens.
- Flag every open question or gap honestly instead of filling it.
