## Owner direction (04:04)
The owner suggests the slip can be a screen built into the machine: an instrument display on the bench, in the device's own light, showing the founder's genes as live readouts. Start there.

The owner frames it as engagement: this is where the game tells the player how they are changing their own mibi. So the game designer co-owns this ticket. They answer what should feel rewarding in a change (seeing the part change on the founder, before against after, a rare look, what is still hidden and tempting) and what the machine's screen does to make that felt. UI/UX gives it the layout.

## Owner ruling (04:06, verbatim)
"a paper tag is not interactive, it makes no sense having a paper tag that changes with user interaction." So the paper tag is out. The slip is a screen of the machine. Rule for every screen: anything that changes with the player's input is the machine's display, never paper or print.

## Purpose
The slip tells the player what they changed, what clashes, and what is still a surprise, on Create and again on the Incubator (pictures 11 and 15). The concept called it "real paper with a metal clip" (specs/create-concept.md). As drawn, it is a cream text box: it reads as neither paper nor the machine, and it is a list of words. The owner has already rejected cream cards with lots of text (13:26).
## Style
design/art-direction.md, design/style-guide/README.md (the Station is rugged, matte field gear; the sage frame). Picture-led, little text (owner 13:26). The purpose-first rule.
## Creative director's input
`specs/slip-visual-intent.md`: a screen of the machine, a matte instrument display let into the bench (and the Incubator's base), in the Caddy e-paper panel's language. It carries picture chips with the drums' own marks (notch, frost, red link), at most 3 words. The paper tag is withdrawn (owner 04:06). Gate checks 8 (words load), 9 (what it is in the world) and 10 (material matches behaviour) apply: `specs/gate-recalibration.md` §§4–5.
## Steps
1. With the creative director: state the visual intent in one sentence (what this object is in the world). For example, a readout built into the bench, or a tag on the pod. Then give it a structure, one row per trait: the trait's picture chip, its state (changed by you / as the pod is / still a surprise, shown as a frosted chip rather than a faint word), and the clash marked on the two drums and their rows, not as a red sentence. Words only where a picture can't say it: the target is that a glance, not reading, tells the player what changed. Set a hard words budget and meet it.
2. Make one design that serves both Create and the Incubator ("cleared while growing" is a state of the same rows).
3. A 1x wireframe of each screen with the new slip, rendered to PNG with its label inside the image. Then amend specs/station-create-bench-spec.md, the Incubator B spec and tickets T-71 and T-123, and write the art ticket.

## Scope widened: the genome field is the machine's screen
The lost reference is `art/references/genome-field/` (01 field overview, 05 local before/after, 06 inspection; README there). It was never carried into the Create or Incubator briefs. It is now the starting point for this ticket. The slip stops being a separate box: Create's and the Incubator's main display is the founder's genome drawn as a field, on the lab machine's own screen.
- From 01: regions (body plan, markings, senses, behaviour…) as clustered neighbourhoods; each locus a node with its two copies; known, carried, unknown and changed states shown by the node's shape, never by a sentence; focus by the corner brackets.
- From 05: a change expands in place, before against after, with the rest of the field still visible.
- From 06: detail (letters, the rule, the reference look) opens on request and returns to the same spot.
- Also two owner-shared third-party references in the internal repo (references/ui, 2026-10-09; never copied here). A skill tree: "the layout is clean, legible, shows progression, have panels with illustrations. good balance." A logic-gate circuit: "showing how DNA is spliced and mixed in a cross, with all the loci at play visible at a glance." The lead describes them on request.
- Keep: the Station style (sage frame, matte field gear, the lab); the purpose-first rule; gate checks 8–10. Out: the old images' palette, knob, resource names and text panels.
- Decided rules that still hold: research must be intensely visual for children, deep genetics optional (10-01); the locus is abstracted from the player (09-28). So letters and codes live only in the opened detail, never on the field. station-gate `design/style-guide/station-layouts.md` "Never on the page" governs the Library's book page, not this screen.
- Who: game designer (what the field shows, what feels rewarding to change, how locked, configurable and self-changing loci read differently) and UI/UX (layout, states), with the creative director on what it is in the world. Architect consulted on drawing a field in LVGL at 1024×600 before the wireframes are final.
